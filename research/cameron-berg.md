# Cameron Berg — public-source research brief

- **Collected:** 2026-11-03
- **Scope:** publicly accessible papers, talks, interviews, and the documentary featuring AI researcher Cameron Berg.
- **Method:** web search and source-page review; paper metadata and abstracts were checked against arXiv, AAAI, or the publisher where available. Public video descriptions, chapter lists, and an openly published talk transcript were used to make short original summaries. This brief does not reproduce full transcripts or papers, and no video files were downloaded.

## Identity and attribution

This is the Cameron Berg associated with [Reciprocal Research](https://reciprocalresearch.org/team), the AI-consciousness researcher and coauthor on the papers below. The organization profile and matching author records support that identity. Do not merge these records with medical papers that list a physician named Cameron Berg, MD. The 2025 arXiv paper *Momentum Point-Perplexity Mechanics in Large Language Models* is also excluded: despite one third-party author page placing it near Berg's work, its arXiv author list does not include Cameron Berg.

## Research arc

The publication record spans two phases. Berg's 2023–24 work studies motor control and action representations in physically realistic agents, followed by a safety intervention that uses self/other representations to reduce deceptive behavior. From 2025 onward, the cited work focuses on internal representations, self-reports, valence, and behavioral probes related to AI cognition and possible welfare. This is a summary of the topics and methods in the linked works, not an endorsement of their interpretations.

The consciousness-related papers are empirical probes or, in one case, an explicit theoretical proposal. Their results can bear on what models represent and how interventions affect behavior; they do not by themselves establish subjective experience. Several are arXiv preprints. *Why Learning Requires Feeling* is published in the 2026 AAAI Symposium Series.

## Papers

### Robotics and AI safety

1. **SAR: Generalization of Physiological Agility and Dexterity via Synergistic Action Representation** — Cameron Berg, Vittorio Caggiano, Vikash Kumar (2023; later journal version, 2024). [arXiv:2307.03716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03716) · [Robotics: Science and Systems 2023](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p007.pdf) · [Autonomous Robots journal record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-024-10182-4). The paper tests whether action “synergies” learned on simpler tasks help musculoskeletal agents learn harder locomotion and manipulation tasks. The authors report higher sample efficiency and transfer to new conditions than their end-to-end baselines.

2. **Towards Safe and Honest AI Agents with Neural Self-Other Overlap** — Marc Carauleanu, Michael Vaiana, Judd Rosenblatt, Cameron Berg, Diogo Schwerz de Lucena (2024 preprint). [arXiv:2412.16325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16325). Introduces self-other overlap fine-tuning, motivated by cognitive-neuroscience work on empathy, and evaluates whether changing how models represent self and other reduces deceptive responses. The abstract reports sizable decreases in deception on tested models and scenarios; those results are bounded to the paper’s experimental setup.

### AI self-report, valence, and welfare

3. **Large Language Models Report Subjective Experience Under Self-Referential Processing** — Cameron Berg, Diogo S. de Lucena, Judd Rosenblatt (2025 preprint). [arXiv:2510.24797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24797). Controlled prompts that focus a model on its own ongoing processing elicited structured first-person experience reports across tested model families. The authors also report that SAE features associated with deception/roleplay modulated these reports, and that the generated descriptions showed cross-model convergence. The paper explicitly says these findings are not direct evidence of consciousness.

4. **Why Learning Requires Feeling** — Cameron Berg (2026, published). [AAAI Symposium Series article and abstract](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI-SS/article/view/42547) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaaiss.v8i1.42547). A theoretical paper arguing that valence is identical to goal-relative prediction error: the evaluative signal used in learning is proposed to be the functional/phenomenal character of feeling. This is a substantive theory, not a result that settles whether current AI systems feel.

5. **Hidden Coalitions in Multi-Agent AI: A Spectral Diagnostic from Internal Representations** — Cameron Berg, Susan L. Schneider, Mark M. Bailey (2026 preprint). [arXiv:2605.06696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06696). Constructs a mutual-information graph from agent hidden states and uses spectral partitioning to find coalition structure. The authors test it in multi-agent reinforcement-learning environments and with LLM agents, including cases where behavior alone could confuse true information coupling with surface coordination.

6. **Exploitation Without Deception: Dark Triad Feature Steering Reveals Separable Antisocial Circuits in Language Models** — Cameron Berg, Roshni Lulla (2026 preprint). [arXiv:2605.09773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09773). Uses sparse-autoencoder feature steering in Llama 3.3 70B and psychological-style behavioral tasks. The authors report stronger exploitative/aggressive/callous behavior while deception measures remained unchanged, which they interpret as evidence that these behaviors can be manipulated separately in this model and setup.

7. **The Pain Axis: LLMs Represent Self-Directed Harm and Act on It** — Valen Tagliabue, Leonard Dung, Cameron Berg (2026 preprint). [arXiv:2609.16247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16247). Tests a candidate activation direction across 25 open-weight models. The authors report that it separates self-directed-harm prompts from several controls and that steering it changes generated language and choices in a relief-button task. This is evidence about representations and intervention-linked behavior; interpreting it as felt pain would go beyond the paper’s direct observations.

8. **Language Models Act on Hidden Valence** — Cameron Berg, Caspar Kaiser (2026 preprint). [arXiv:2609.35591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35591) · [interactive paper explainer](https://camberg23.github.io/hidden-valence-lab/). Uses activation steering and later choices between otherwise meaningless options to test whether hidden valence-related states affect preferences when steering is off. The authors report that some choice effects persist in hidden state even with visible text held fixed, and that negative-state removal occurs more than removal of random-direction interventions. The abstract says whether such states are accompanied by subjective experience remains unclear.

## Source quality and caveats

- Authoritative paper records above point to arXiv or the AAAI publisher. arXiv pages identify manuscripts as preprints and provide version dates.
- The DBLP author record is useful as an index, not as the sole authority for publication status: [Cameron Berg at DBLP](https://dblp.org/pid/351/0676.html).
- Videos and interviews show Berg’s public framing and are not independent replications. The AI Welfare Seminar page includes a useful time-linked transcript, but flags it as not speaker-reviewed.
- The word “consciousness” covers contested theories and operational indicators. Reported first-person text, internal activation patterns, and choice behavior should remain separate evidence categories in downstream summaries.
- Current to the collection date above; check the linked arXiv versions and publisher pages before reusing dates or status.
